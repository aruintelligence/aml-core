ĀML™ — ĀRU Meaning Language™

What if an AI interface had to explain why it was allowed to exist?

ĀML is a working research prototype for making the meaning, policy, authority, provenance, and evidence behind AI-generated interfaces more inspectable before and after rendering.

HTML tells the browser what to display. ĀML tells the system why it deserves to be displayed.
Run the live proof Open the GitHub repository Open View Meaning™

The category: AI interface accountability

Generative and agentic systems can now select, assemble, personalize, rank, or generate human-facing interfaces dynamically. Traditional frontend stacks are excellent at representation and rendering, but the pixels alone may not tell an evaluator what purpose was declared, which policy ran, what authority existed, or why output was allowed or suppressed.

ĀML explores an AI Interface Firewall™: a decision boundary between machine/app intent and the rendered interface.

AML and HTML are different layers

AML does not require teams to abandon HTML, CSS, JavaScript, React, Next.js, or browser infrastructure. A useful mental model is:

ĀML = meaning + policy + accountability decision layer
HTML = rendering and interface substrate

The relevant question is not whether AML replaces HTML. It is whether AI-generated interfaces benefit from explicit, machine-readable meaning and evidence around the decision to render.

Independent verification is part of the design

AML publishes a claims ledger, verification guide, conformance fixtures, RFCs, schemas, protocol vectors, security documentation, public challenge paths, and independent implementation challenges. PASS, FAIL, and MIXED reports can all improve the specification when they are reproducible.
