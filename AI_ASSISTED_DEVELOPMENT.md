AI assistance has been used across the project lifecycle, including activities such as:

- comparing interoperability approaches;
- drafting documentation and technical publications;
- examining release, package, and verification workflows;
- adversarially reviewing claims and evidence boundaries.

Human authorship, project direction, repository ownership, release decisions, branding, and responsibility for what is published remain with the project author/steward.

## What this disclosure does not mean

AI-assisted development does **not** imply endorsement, partnership, certification, ownership, sponsorship, or standards approval by OpenAI or by any other AI provider whose tools may have been used.

It also does not make generated code automatically correct. That is why the repository uses automated tests, conformance checks, release-coherence gates, package audits, cryptographic verification, external-verifier challenges, and explicit evidence boundaries.

## Why disclose it

ĀML is partly about making machine intent and evidence inspectable. Hiding the role of AI in building an AI-accountability project would work against that principle.

The relevant standard here is transparency: disclose the assistance, preserve human responsibility, and make the resulting technical claims independently testable.
